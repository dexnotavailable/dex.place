Exact dialogue, character designs, intermediate rooms and staging remain design work. This is planning approval, not an implementation go or a new site title. ## Confirmed constraints

Dex likes the quieter arrival study and the aesthetics of Open Atrium/Courtyard Between. Arrival should be spacious and still; denser, stranger places can appear later. The world uses familiar spaces in impossible relationships, with an exploratory atmosphere rather than horror. The account registry table is near the start. Arena boss kills lead to product menus for in-world downloads, archive access has no prerequisite, and the exhibit takes a longer scenic journey. Donation boxes have nearby/easy top-donor views; there is no treasury. The complete website remains accessible by scrolling, regardless of progress or account state.

## Accepted premise

Rooms sometimes become disconnected from the buildings they belonged to. They arrive here intact: a classroom, a public pool, an apartment landing, a waiting room. A small staff gives them addresses and keeps the paths between them usable.

The enormous arrival hall is the registry's receiving space. Its furnishings are ordinary because the people working here are ordinary-sized. The wider architecture is impossible. The floating ring remains a landmark whose purpose is not explained by this first story.

This is a working place, not an abandoned ruin or a world that needs saving. There are few residents, and their routines continue even when nobody is visiting. Most of the scenery stays still. A displaced room is a rare event, not a reason for constant visual reshuffling or procedural disorientation.

## The visitor

The player is a traveller who has arrived here, with no imposed personal tragedy, chosen-one role, identity reveal or mandatory backstory. They can browse, fight, look around, or continue to the website. Talking to the accountant does not require making a dex account. Account registration is an optional real service at the desk, distinct from story progress.

The visitor has a sword because physical encounters are part of this world, but carrying it does not make every interaction a sword action. Doors, records and artwork use inspect/use; attacks have a physical target and consequence.

## First story: a courtyard with the wrong connection

1. Arrival gets its quiet first frame: the large opening, centrally placed traveller and distant form. Latest steering moves the desk and donation point beyond the first right-hand reveal, still near the start; a short left branch reveals a resting place. No incident, service content, dialogue or tutorial runs automatically over the scenic opening. Movement is available once the visitor enters the ready world. See the central-arrival floor-plan recommendation for proposed camera boundaries and connections.
2. Slightly beyond the initial framing, an ordinary door is marked Courtyard. Opening or inspecting it reveals open sky through the doorway, with a safe foreground landing. This is a spatial discrepancy, not an untelegraphed lethal fall.
3. The accountant knows the courtyard is still here. A distinctive muted-red handrail/tree shape can be glimpsed higher through the architecture. One short optional exchange or gesture is enough; there is no mandatory explanation or quest panel.
4. The scenic branch passes through a low corridor, one familiar interior and an exposed crossing before reaching the exhibit beside that courtyard. Their materials and landmarks establish that this is one place. The exact intermediate rooms remain a map-design choice.
5. On the courtyard side, the visitor releases a simple maintenance latch and opens the return doorway. This reconnects to the safe arrival-side doorway beside the registry. The mechanism is physically operated, not automatically unlocked by opening an art menu, signing in or donating.
6. Returning through it creates recognition: the visitor is back at the small desk, with the route they just explored now connected. The accountant uses the reopened courtyard threshold for an ordinary break, such as moving a cup/chair into the light. Keep account service available at the desk. The first arc ends through a small visible change rather than a completion banner.

This proposes a roughly three-to-five-minute optional scenic loop for an interested first visitor, not a timed sequence or a promise about final pacing. The first minute should already feel rewarding, and the direct arena route remains shorter. Opening the return is personal exploration progress; it does not alter other visitors' encounters or require a globally shared world state.

## Accepted repeat-boss logic

Dispatch consignments are held by seals that form physical wardens. To release a selected consignment through the world, destroy its warden in the arena. Each new collection forms another seal/warden, so a fresh encounter is part of the institution's strange routine.

The warden is an animated construct; its destruction is real, and a later encounter is a fresh construct rather than repeatedly killing a friendly resident. One reused arena and one properly authored guardian can serve the first version. This does not require procedural bosses, a boss roster per product, or a new magical combat system.

Defeat -> restrained death beat -> black transition -> selected product menu remains the flow. The narrative does not permit an automatic file download, and direct website downloads stay immediate. Boss death should feel tactile rather than being an excuse to display software UI. The public labels can remain plain Downloads and Documentation even if the fictional location has a different everyday name.

## Proposed cast and tone

- Accountant: recurring anchor, practical and quietly attentive. They know room numbers and routines; they are not an omniscient exposition source. Their response to the restored connection supplies the first emotional payoff.
- Optional exhibit attendant: a second resident with a visible ordinary activity. Add only if their presence improves the selected gallery scene; the first story works without them.
- Warden: combat construct, no dialogue. Silhouette/materials should belong to the selected setting and actual animation production plan.

Humour comes from matter-of-fact behaviour in impossible circumstances. Do not turn all dialogue into riddles, philosophical slogans or winking software jokes. Use environmental details and short optional exchanges. The gallery does not acquire invented lore about Dex's real illustrations.

## Production implications to resolve after story approval

- First two doors share a recognisable frame/mark. Need closed/open/latch states, safe threshold geometry and corresponding destination framing.
- Distant courtyard identifier must be recognisable again on approach; the generated scene should establish that sightline before individual pieces are extracted.
- A few purposeful accountant states are needed: work/idle, acknowledge or gesture, small end-of-loop response. Existing idle/walk donors do not prove these clips exist.
- Full room compositions precede functional layer extraction and animation, including background behind the opening door and any moving furniture. No bulk character or room generation starts merely because this text has been drafted.
- The first arc must not require a cinematic camera takeover, a compulsory cutscene, a quest HUD, inventory management or a collection checklist. The intended reward is a usable connection and a visible change in routine.
