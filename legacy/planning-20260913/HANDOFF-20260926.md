## The actual product Dex wants

`dex.place` should be Dex's home on this machine and on the web: a place to get projects and downloads, read documentation, see Dex's own illustrations, donate, and understand the breadth of what he makes. It must be a useful website even for someone who does not play. The four primary content destinations are **Downloads, Documentation, Illustrations, Donate**. A separate `dex account` can eventually bind the site to dexCode, dexClient and future Dex products. A visitor should be able to scroll out of the game into a full, naturally scrollable website with direct URLs, navigation, readable articles and immediate access to downloads. The boss is a ritual for the **world route**, never a gate on direct web access.

The first impression is **arrival**. Show honest loading, then one deliberate Enter action with an up-front sound choice. The same gesture gives the visitor control and starts the chosen sound state; it must not require an extra click or attack inadvertently. Dissolve into a wide, still 2D pixel-art space with the small traveller centrally composed, a safe ground band, drifting cloud layers and an immense, partly cropped distant ring/structure. No NPC, donation point, category list, sales copy or quest text should occupy that first view. There is no forced timer to admire it. A thin muted-red floor inlay suggests right; going left leads to a short shaded overlook/rest. The account desk is close but revealed only after walking right. The opening should induce awe, even a little megalophobia: ordinary near-scale rails/doors/furniture against impossible distant architecture and generous empty space.

The experience should feel **cinematic, quiet, exploratory, emotionally resonant and fun to move through**. Dex accepted the spacious Open Atrium/Courtyard Between direction. His photo references showed a tiny person against an enormous submerged creature, a remote upright mass and a vast rounded form; their lesson is proportion, silhouette, depth and negative space, not those exact subjects. The newer influences are familiar-but-wrong Backrooms space and Monogatari-like odd framing, with anime sensibility in the atmosphere and inhabitants. This is not an abandoned horror maze. Hollow Knight is principally a reference for tactile sword combat, control and connected exploration, not an art style or a source of characters. Bruno Simon's portfolio is the reference for making interaction an enjoyable way into useful site content, not for copying a car or 3D engine.

Brand: the visible mark is literally lowercase **`dex` in the Daniel wordmark font**. No tagline, “solo dev,” “made with love,” empty subtitle, role label, corporate sales copy or mysterious one-liner intended to sell the visitor on Dex. Short text should either help someone use a service or give a real character a small voice. Pixel headings may use a separate readable pixel font; long docs, account details, bank facts and accessibility text must remain legible.

## Accepted fiction: The Registry

The Registry is a small working institution for rooms that have become disconnected from their original buildings. Rooms arrive intact; a few ordinary-sized staff give them addresses and maintain the paths. It is inhabited but sparse. Staff continue normal work; nobody explains the impossible architecture in a monologue. The enormous ring is a landmark, not a mystery solved by chapter one. The traveller has a sword and freedom to explore, with no compulsory trauma, chosen-one identity or quest biography. Dex's character **Kaizen belongs to the separate Convergence story** and should not silently become the site's traveller or environmental canon. Dex's personal illustrations are gallery content only, never texture, scenery, sprite, marketing backdrop or image-generation reference. Their descriptions and Convergence story can be authored later with Dex.

The first small story is a courtyard whose door is wrong. Near the registry, a marked Courtyard doorway opens onto safe sky rather than the expected room. The accountant matter-of-factly remembers the red rail and one tree. Taking the longer scenic route brings the visitor to the actual courtyard. The visitor physically releases a far-side maintenance latch; its paired doorway now returns near the accountant. On returning, the accountant makes a small, **anchored** acknowledgement and takes a cup break facing the newly connected light. This change in routine and useful shortcut are the payoff. No completion banner, lore dump, donation, sign-in or art-menu click can substitute for operating the latch. Later chapters and the ring's meaning are open.

Dispatch uses seals that form constructed wardens. A selected in-world product/consignment summons a fresh warden each visit. Kill it through an authored fight; finish the death beat, cut to black, then open the correct product menu. **The visitor chooses Download explicitly.** Closing the visit or choosing another product means another fight later. Past wins do not permanently unlock the world route. The normal website route always offers direct files. For the current first product, the reward is the site's real documentation ZIP; do not invent dexCode binaries merely to fill the list.

## Spatial grammar and actual first chapter

The big illustrated map is a connection and landmark anchor, **never a measurement diagram**. Each room is composed as its own wide scene and camera. The first build contains these twelve spaces and branches:

| Route | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Rest overlook ↔ central Arrival ↔ Registry | Scenic first frame; optional quiet left reward; human services beyond the right reveal. |
| Registry ↔ Junction ↔ Dispatch vestibule ↔ Arena | Short clear main route for downloads. Encounter entry is deliberate from a safe vestibule. |
| Junction ↔ Archive ↔ Registry | Lower loop to ordinary ungated documentation. The archive has a real door, not a puzzle lock. |
| Junction upper route ↔ Low/service passage ↔ Reflecting pool ↔ Sky walk ↔ Exhibit ↔ Courtyard | Longer journey with compression, water/interior quiet, an exposed crossing, individual artwork and the red rail/tree recognition. |
| Courtyard → Registry after the latch | Personal restored shortcut; returns beside the desk, not to the initial spawn or arena. |

The rest branch should terminate visibly, and the upper/scenic branch should turn back across the map rather than becoming one more rightward hallway. Repeated views of the same distant structure and recognizable paired doors orient the player even when connections are impossible. Safe arrival, registry, archive and inspection spaces need air and human scale. A few authored mob pockets can make later routes dangerous; sparse life and surprise encounters work better than crowding every chamber. Damage includes knockback and death, followed by nearby safe recovery. Social ghosts, if later enabled, do not share combat, boss progress or mechanism state.

The main priority order from Dex was downloads, donate, illustrations, documentation. That is a **wayfinding/service priority**, not four rooms arranged in one compulsory corridor. The exhibit may take more of a journey because attachment grows with the approach. Donation boxes can appear at sensible safe spots; an easy adjacent donor record supports recognition. Dex explicitly scrapped a separate treasury room. Never fabricate donor ranks, payment success or contribution totals.

## Interaction language

- **Use / E**: inspect an individual artwork, document, door, desk, donation box, donor record, latch or terminal. Open an intentional panel with focus, close/back behavior and a return to the world. An artwork opens its own inspector; a separate catalogue opens the broader folder. The archive is ordinary and accessible. At overlapping stair mouths, a single E selects the flight, followed by continuous walking rather than E on every step.
- **Slash / click / J**: sword action with visible contact, anticipation and consequence. A reachable jump-and-slash cuts the map restraint; the banner physically unrolls from a fixed top rod, then E inspects a full layout. It is not quick travel, and a cut cannot be undone within that save. At the sky walk the sword cuts a real rope: fixed post remains, cord separates, hinged deck lowers and actually becomes a traversable bridge. The latch uses E and rotates only its lever, not its mounting plate.
- **Mobility**: left/right movement, buffered/variable jump, double jump, dash, readable stairs and forgiving collision. Attack and locomotion need to feel tactile. Footsteps should match the surface and not sound like generic clicks. Combat uses readable wind-up, impact and recovery; boss death cannot be a technical trigger with no visual end.
- **Touch**: Samsung Internet on Android and iPad Safari are the primary real devices. Touch movement and actions must work together. Test each integrated revision on mobile; emulation and real-device observations are separate evidence.
- **Presentation**: world UI is restrained, with black cinematic bars and small nearby prompts. In-world menus should have material and typography that belong to the setting, while remaining real accessible DOM for text, focus, buttons, sliders, forms and QR. When the eventual visitor scrolls down, the bars recede and a real web layout takes over. Do not squeeze the entire useful website into a game modal or force game navigation on people who want a download.

See [GAME-INTERACTIONS.md](GAME-INTERACTIONS.md) for each currently implemented button/panel and the distinction between Use and Slash. The lower website's full account and scroll contracts are historical planning in the old site docs; reconcile them with this newer game before implementing that phase.

## Visual-production philosophy and failure lessons

The first website proved that a side-view world could work, but Dex judged its foundation only “okay.” The early small-pieces-first image workflow produced unrelated props, repeated platform modules that looked like Lego, perspective conflicts, SVG stand-ins, vague interactables, one-direction travel, janky stairs and abrupt audio. It was possible to satisfy every line of a written spec while the integrated site still felt bad. Preserve the specs as guardrails and change an asset, layout or rule when its **actual scene** fails. Passing technical gates never overrides taste.

The corrected workflow is **whole-room concept first**. Establish the entire twelve-room graph and shared landmark, then render each distinct complete room as a composition. Use that accepted room to derive clear foreground walkable structure, midground, distant background, stationary housings, moving pieces, emissive/light sprites and animation sheets. A source image may guide separation or prompt a new component. The final runtime cannot be one flattened scene or one giant all-purpose sheet. Concealed areas behind doors/objects must exist. Every prop should have a reason to be there and a state: fixed versus moving parts, idle/contact/settled frames, bounds, pivots, depth and scale. Test a scene early, then repeat at all rooms. Asset count around fifty was an inventory sanity check, never an aesthetic target; the current catalogue has seventy registered items.

Make the world **unmistakably pixel art** after actual crop, scale and camera rendering. Nearby small objects may need chunkier pixels; huge platforms need finer source texture so they do not look like toy blocks; distant forms can be finer still. Use a coherent apparent pixel pitch, silhouette, orientation and flat side-view geometry. Avoid a 3D or isometric-looking walkway when the camera will never rotate. Higher-definition source art followed by controlled, object-specific pixel reduction was allowed as an experiment, but no global mosaic filter should flatten the result. Generated animation must be judged at its intended screen size through a full cycle; individual pretty frames do not prove coherent motion. Lamp housing and its glow are separate; cloud shapes have actual frame animation as well as slow parallax; water has real frames plus matching reflected sky. The main hero remains the inspected CC0 Martial Hero baseline for now. Original authored character animation remains a later aspiration.

Use the built-in image route as the normal asset path. Earlier Higgsfield model experiments did not justify replacing the stable CC0 player and should be treated as historical research, not an automatic expenditure. The built-in tool does not prove a named backend model merely because it generated an image. Art-production originals, rejected variants, prompts, keying/crop reports and hashes are preserved under `D:/Dex/Projects/dex-place-art-production/registry-game-20260913/`. Historical free asset/code donors and design references are catalogued in `site/docs/world-website/23-25`; verify current licences and scene fit before any new reuse. Important research roles: Team Cherry for connected routes/shortcuts and residents, Journey for scale and chance encounters, Eastward for the warm refuge/material connection, Bruno Simon for play leading into usable information, Celeste physics/fairness and animation references for contact and readable timing. These are **different questions**, not a combined style palette.

## Audio target

Dex rejected an energetic opening and a stitched comparison that jumped abruptly between zones. He asked for something slower, more emotional and memorable: an identifiable higher-register piano gesture, a little silence and negative space at the beginning, then restrained strings. An original 16-bar idea at 56 BPM in D minor around A–D–E–F was used as a guide for a Suno Cover experiment. Dex chose the arrangement at approximately54 seconds of the comparison reel, the more grand/mystical **Suno B** version. The game reuses that full selected cue and a restrained arena variant. Ambience should arrive before score; music should swell rather than start abruptly, remain calm during travel, gain some orchestral weight in the arena, and leave certain archive/art/reading moments quiet. Compatible rooms should keep musical continuity rather than restarting the track at every door. Steps and effects need a listening check too.

Dex wants control and atmosphere more than award-level generation. Preserve ElevenLabs credits for other media by default; no new ElevenLabs spend is required for this site pass. Do not copy any pasted key from chat into a handoff, source or prompt. The current build's audio envelope has technical scheduling proof but **no fresh human listening approval**. Any next audio revision should be based on an audible comparison with Dex and must label costs/provider rights before new generation.
