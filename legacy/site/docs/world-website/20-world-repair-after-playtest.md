## Repair priorities

1. **Distinct places.** Rebuild the world as separate chambers and thresholds. Each destination needs an entrance, enclosure or open vista, its own focal object and room to breathe. The old 3840-wide map exposes several destination props in one view; spreading a few props by tens of pixels is insufficient. Keep direct tabs available. Spread useful physical documentation around the things it explains; the Archive remains the full document index.
2. **A coherent interaction kit.** Audit intact, contact, releasing, cut and settled states together. Cables must visibly attach to mounts and a meaningful mechanism. Cutting must leave believable attached and released pieces, with no unexplained floating hook or disconnected deck. Reuse the useful parts of the existing library; replace weak parts rather than forcing all 50 into the scene.
3. **Continuous traversal.** Replace the exposed diagonal stair web with short contained changes of level and deliberate lift transitions. A normal walking input must ascend/descend the intended stair route without repeated jumps, snagging or micro-falls. Authored visible treads and the physical walking surface must agree.
4. **Restrained foley and score.** Fix stair grounding before tuning foot contacts. Walking must not fire repeated landing sounds on each tread. Footsteps should sound like a small traveler in this space, with quiet material variation and appropriate cadence. Build ordinary regions from one calm musical vocabulary; do not join independently energetic cues just because they share a nominal BPM/key. Silence is an authored state, not a missing audio file. Arena orchestration is a later variant, not the opening atmosphere.
5. **Real integrated review.** First demonstrate two distinct connected spaces, one mechanically coherent cut, normal stair/lift traversal and the calm/silent transition. Inspect their static frame and motion at desktop and phone size before expanding. Then inspect the whole route. Technical passes support this review but never overrule a poor experience.
